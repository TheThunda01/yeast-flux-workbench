# Assumptions and their consequences

The calculation asks what a fixed network permits under declared constraints. Biological agreement requires a separate comparison with measurements matched to strain, medium and units.

The register separates evidence about the input from choices made for this analysis. “Checked” means only the stated check passed; it does not establish experimental validity.

| Assumption | Why it is used and what supports it | Check and consequence if it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The exact yeast-GEM v9.1.1 snapshot is the input. | A pinned community reconstruction makes the analysis reproducible; Zhang et al. (2024) supply model context, not validation of this later release. | Checked: SHA-256, dimensions, identifiers, objective and selected bounds. Snapshot counts are measured from this file. Completeness of biological knowledge remains untested here. |
| S288C and the encoded reaction/compartment assignments are suitable context. | They are upstream model choices rather than properties inferred by this workbench. | No new strain-specific experiment is compared. Applying the model to another strain or environment requires a new assessment. |
| All balanced metabolites satisfy Sv=0. | Conventional FBA studies steady-state flux feasibility (Orth et al., 2010), without fitting concentration trajectories. | Every successful solution has residual checks. Transient accumulation or omitted reactions would invalidate a literal biological reading despite numerical balance. |
| Maximizing r_2111 is the question of interest. | It measures the best biomass drain permitted by the encoded network, using upstream biomass assembly and normalization. | The objective and drain are checked; r_4041 supplies assembly. Real cells need not maximize growth, and other objectives would yield different answers. |
| Biomass composition and maintenance stay fixed. | The snapshot’s composition and r_4046=0.7 permit a controlled comparison of only two exchange bounds. They are inherited model assumptions, not new estimates. | Maintenance is checked in code. Sensitivity to biomass/maintenance is not implemented; a mismatch can change growth and feasibility, including the no-glucose result. |
| The retained deposited bounds define the intended background. | Changing only glucose and oxygen makes scenario differences interpretable. The official minimal_Y6 preset documents related medium choices. | Selected initial bounds and hashes are checked; all other bounds are retained. This is not a separately validated medium or a fresh application of every preset instruction. |
| Selected exchange signs mean negative uptake. | The exact glucose/oxygen columns each have coefficient −1 for their exchanged metabolite. | Code checks these columns and lower bounds. Sign errors would reverse the biological interpretation of a cap. |
| Positive oxygen caps are sufficient for this narrow scenario sweep. | A zero-oxygen physiological model needs additional choices; the official anaerobic preset changes more than oxygen. | The interface rejects zero oxygen. This does not prove that positive oxygen is actually taken up or that the scenario reproduces aerobic physiology. |
| FVA growth fractions 1, 0.99 and 0.9 are useful sensitivity settings. | They are prespecified choices for examining feasible alternatives (Kenefake et al., 2022), not estimated probabilities. | Fractions and reaction membership are checked. Results depend on these settings; separate extrema cannot be combined into an assumed single flux state. |
| The declared growth-floor slack is acceptable for numerical comparisons. | A relaxation of `1e-7*(1+abs(z))` avoids unstable exactly binding floors in this implementation. This is an engineering choice. | Requested/effective floors and violations are exported; the independent algorithm uses the same contract. A 100% target is not exact equality, and tiny extrema can depend on the slack. |
| Ordinary FVA answers a stoichiometric feasibility question. | It deliberately omits loopless, complete thermodynamic, enzyme-capacity and regulatory constraints. Schellenberger et al. (2011) explain the loop caveat. | Artificial-bound flags identify selected cap contacts but do not detect all loops. Some feasible fluxes may be physiologically impossible. |
| The no-glucose case is infeasible under the retained constraints. | It is a configured diagnostic of this model, including its maintenance demand, rather than a universal statement that yeast cannot use other carbon sources. | Actual solver status must match the expectation. An infeasible case has null growth; timeout/unbounded/error cannot be relabeled as infeasibility. |
| Numerical tolerances and a 30-second solve limit are appropriate locally. | They balance strict residual checks and practical runtime for this fixed sparse problem. | Primal/dual residuals, bounds and objective gaps are checked; another HiGHS algorithm repeats optima and ranges. Agreement is numerical, not independent biology. |
| The output folder is complete and corresponds to its inputs. | Saved full FBA vectors and hashes make results inspectable. | Verification checks vectors, membership and table agreement. Per-file replacement can leave partial output after interruption; integrity checks cannot authenticate authorship or independently critique the prose. |

The [references](references.md) separate the published model and methods from this workbench’s calculations. No empirical flux variability, prediction interval, Crabtree-effect validation or dynamic-growth prediction is claimed.
