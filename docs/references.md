# References and their roles

These sources support the model background and interpretation. They do not establish that this workbench agrees with a new experiment. The [literature review](literature-review.md) and [search log](search-log.json) record discovery and appraisal limits; the [methods](methods.md) describe what the implementation actually does.

## Model and biological context

- **Zhang C et al. (2024).** Yeast9: a consensus genome-scale metabolic model for *S. cerevisiae* curated by the community. *Molecular Systems Biology* 20:1134–1150. [DOI: 10.1038/s44320-024-00060-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44320-024-00060-7); [PMID 39134886](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39134886/). Complete PMC text was retrieved and relevant model/limitation sections appraised. This is the primary description of Yeast9, not a claim that its published version or validation is identical to the later v9.1.1 snapshot used here. In particular, publication and snapshot reaction, metabolite, and gene counts should not be substituted for one another.
- **Lu H et al. (2019).** A consensus *S. cerevisiae* metabolic model Yeast8 and its ecosystem for comprehensively probing cellular metabolism. *Nature Communications* 10:3586. [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-019-11581-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11581-3); [PMID 31395883](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31395883/). The citation and abstract were checked for the resource's development and version-control context. Detailed predictions from its derived models are not adopted here.
- **Moreno-Paz S, Schmitz J, Martins Dos Santos VAP, Suarez-Diez M (2022).** Enzyme-constrained models predict the dynamics of *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* growth in continuous, batch and fed-batch bioreactors. *Microbial Biotechnology* 15:1434–1445. [DOI: 10.1111/1751-7915.13995](https://doi.org/10.1111/1751-7915.13995); [PMID 35048533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35048533/). Complete PMC text was retrieved; relevant enzyme-capacity, dynamic-model, and limitation sections were appraised. Its comparisons explain why conventional FBA should not inherit claims about dynamic behavior or the Crabtree effect. Its parameterization and numerical results are not reproduced here.

## Constraint-based methods

- **Orth JD, Thiele I, Palsson BØ (2010).** What is flux balance analysis? *Nature Biotechnology* 28:245–248. [DOI: 10.1038/nbt.1614](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.1614); [PMID 20212490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20212490/). The complete PMC primer was read. It supports stoichiometric steady-state balance, flux bounds, objective functions, biomass pseudoreactions, and the distinction between fluxes and concentrations. Its worked organism/example is not this yeast analysis.
- **Kenefake D, Armingol E, Lewis NE, Pistikopoulos EN (2022).** An improved algorithm for flux variability analysis. *BMC Bioinformatics* 23:550. [DOI: 10.1186/s12859-022-05089-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-022-05089-9); [PMID 36536290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36536290/). Complete PMC text was retrieved and the FVA formulation appraised. It supports conditional feasible ranges and an objective-retention factor. The workbench uses the conventional separate minimum/maximum problems, not this paper's acceleration algorithm.
- **Schellenberger J, Lewis NE, Palsson BØ (2011).** Elimination of thermodynamically infeasible loops in steady-state metabolic models. *Biophysical Journal* 100:544–553. [DOI: 10.1016/j.bpj.2010.12.3707](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2010.12.3707); [PMID 21281568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21281568/). Citation and abstract were checked; the full-text connector returned an internal error. The abstract supports the narrow warning that ordinary steady-state flux solutions can violate loop constraints. No detailed algorithm claim or loopless implementation is made.

## Exact upstream material and software

The model snapshot is the official [yeast-GEM v9.1.1 release](https://github.com/SysBioChalmers/yeast-GEM/releases/tag/v9.1.1), commit `2d594ae1c4a2d550ccef120d96a58c7bbf586255`. The primary model file is [model/yeast-GEM.mat](https://github.com/SysBioChalmers/yeast-GEM/blob/2d594ae1c4a2d550ccef120d96a58c7bbf586255/model/yeast-GEM.mat). This snapshot contains 2,748 metabolites, 4,105 reactions, and 1,143 genes as parsed for this project; these are version-specific counts. The source SHA-256 is recorded in repository provenance.

The official [minimal_Y6 medium](https://github.com/SysBioChalmers/yeast-GEM/blob/2d594ae1c4a2d550ccef120d96a58c7bbf586255/data/conditions/minimal_Y6.yml) defines the aerobic baseline context. The separate [anaerobic preset](https://github.com/SysBioChalmers/yeast-GEM/blob/2d594ae1c4a2d550ccef120d96a58c7bbf586255/data/conditions/anaerobic.yml) shows why changing oxygen alone does not reproduce all anaerobic model assumptions. The model's [CC BY 4.0 license](https://github.com/SysBioChalmers/yeast-GEM/blob/2d594ae1c4a2d550ccef120d96a58c7bbf586255/LICENSE.md) is retained separately from the original code license.

[SciPy 1.17.0 `linprog(method="highs")`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.17.0/reference/optimize.linprog-highs.html) documents the linear objective, equality/inequality constraints, explicit bounds, solver statuses, and numerical tolerances. It supports the executable optimization interface and numerical checks, not a biological guarantee. The relevant official page was inspected on 20 October 2026.
